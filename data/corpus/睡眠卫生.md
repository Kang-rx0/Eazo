# 睡眠卫生

CDC sleep health guidance and NIOSH evidence-based tips to improve sleep. Key recommendations for working adults.

## How much sleep adults need

Most adults aged 18-60 need 7 or more hours of good quality, uninterrupted sleep each day. Adults 61-64 need 7-9 hours. Some people need even more, especially during stressful times or after high mental or physical demands.

Getting enough sleep helps you get sick less often, stay at a healthy weight, reduce stress and improve mood, improve heart health and metabolism, lower the risk of type 2 diabetes, heart disease, high blood pressure and stroke, and improve attention and memory.

Signs of poor sleep quality include trouble falling asleep, repeatedly waking up during the night, and feeling sleepy or tired even after getting enough sleep.

## Habits that improve sleep

Go to bed and get up at about the same times every day, including days off. Consistent sleep times improve sleep. Ideally go to bed early enough that you do not need an alarm to wake up.

Turn off electronic devices at least 30 minutes before bedtime. Do not expose your eyes to computer or phone screens near bedtime.

Avoid large meals and alcohol before bedtime. Avoid heavy or spicy meals within 3 hours of your regular bedtime. Alcohol may help you fall asleep but causes sleep disturbances; if you drink, finish several hours before bedtime.

Avoid caffeine in the afternoon or evening: no caffeine, chocolate, or nicotine for 5 or more hours before planned sleep, more if you are sensitive.

Limit liquids several hours before sleep to avoid waking up to go to the bathroom.

## Exercise and daylight help sleep

During the day, get some exercise. Even a 10-minute walk will improve sleep, and more is better. Plan to finish exercise at least 3 hours before planned sleep.

Get bright light during the daytime, ideally 30 minutes or so outside in sunlight. Bright light in the first hours of your day is particularly helpful for strengthening biological rhythms that promote daytime alertness and nighttime sleep.

## Sleep environment

Make the bedroom very dark, quiet, cool and comfortable. Block out lights (especially blue and white lights), use opaque window coverings or an eye mask if needed. Use soft ear plugs if the environment is noisy. Keep a comfortably cool room temperature, about 18-20 degrees Celsius (65-68 F). Do not let pets or phones disturb your sleep. Use the bedroom only for sleep and intimacy; do not watch TV, read, or work in bed.

## Wind-down routine

Follow a relaxing routine about 1.5 hours before bedtime to help the body transition from wakefulness to sleep. Avoid excitement such as action movies or upsetting news. Transition to dim lighting. A warm bath 30 minutes to 2 hours before bedtime can promote relaxation and aid sleep.

Pay attention to your body's cues: if you get very sleepy earlier than usual, go to bed. Drowsiness is the body's way of saying it needs sleep, for example when fighting off an infection or recovering from a demanding day.

## When to seek help

Call your doctor if you spend 7 to 9 hours in bed but consistently take 30 minutes or more to fall asleep, awaken several times or for long periods, take frequent naps, or often feel sleepy at inappropriate times. Common sleep disorders include insomnia, restless legs syndrome, narcolepsy, and sleep apnea.
