# WHO 2020 身体活动指南

World Health Organization 2020 guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour. Key recommendations for adults aged 18-64, including those with chronic conditions.

## Aerobic physical activity for adults

Adults should do at least 150-300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity per week, or at least 75-150 minutes of vigorous-intensity aerobic physical activity, or an equivalent combination of moderate- and vigorous-intensity activity throughout the week, for substantial health benefits.

Physical activity bouts of any length count toward the weekly total. The previous requirement that activity must last at least 10 minutes per bout has been removed: even a few minutes of movement counts.

Doing more than 300 minutes of moderate-intensity activity per week may provide additional health benefits, though the relative gains diminish at higher volumes.

## Muscle-strengthening activities

Adults should also do muscle-strengthening activities at moderate or greater intensity that involve all major muscle groups on 2 or more days a week, as these provide additional health benefits beyond aerobic activity alone.

## Sedentary behaviour

Adults should limit the amount of time spent being sedentary. Prolonged sedentary behaviour is associated with increased all-cause mortality, cardiovascular disease mortality, and cancer risk.

Replacing sedentary time with physical activity of any intensity, including light-intensity activity such as easy walking or standing up and moving around, provides health benefits.

People who sit for long periods (such as office workers) should aim to do more than the recommended levels of moderate- to vigorous-intensity physical activity to help offset the harms of high sedentary time.

## Good-practice statements

Some physical activity is better than none. Adults who do not currently meet the recommendations should start by doing small amounts of physical activity.

Start small and increase gradually: begin with modest amounts of activity and gradually increase frequency, intensity, and duration over time. This is safer and easier to keep up than sudden large increases.

Every move counts: daily activities such as walking, climbing stairs, and household chores all contribute to weekly physical activity totals.
